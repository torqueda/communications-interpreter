Letter Explainer (working title)

A one-screen web app that turns an official English letter into plain Spanish: a side-by-side translation with key sentences color- and icon-matched across both languages, a short summary, and a scam warning when something looks wrong.

The core journey

Preset A, "Clear paper": cool off-white, ink blue, highlighter-marker colors, and Atkinson Hyperlegible at about 18px. Buttons are at least 44px tall, and red is used only for scam warnings. This page uses the same palette.

Features and behavior

Interface language

A language choice on first open with a "switch any time" note. The toggle is fixed in the top-right corner. Switching changes all interface text instantly and keeps the results on screen.

  • First open shows the choice and the note.
  • Switching mid-results keeps the letters and summary.
  • The toggle stays visible after scrolling to the bottom.

Uploading a letter

One PDF, one image, or a .txt file, dragged in or chosen by clicking the area. The upload area is the most prominent element before results. Added in spec: image uploads show a calm caution box explaining that reading a picture is less reliable than real text.

  • A PDF starts processing.
  • A PNG or JPG screenshot starts processing.
  • Image uploads show the image caution box; PDFs don't.

Processing animation

A friendly animation plays while the app works.

  • It appears right after upload and disappears when results or a message appear.

Side-by-side letters with matched highlights

The original is on the left and the Spanish translation on the right, both in full. Key sentences share a color and icon across both columns, without line-by-line alignment.

  • Every translation highlight has a matching highlight in the original.
  • Every highlighted sentence in the original really appears in the uploaded letter.

Highlight interactions

Clicking, tapping, or pressing Enter on a highlight flashes its twin in the other language and shows a short tooltip ("This is your deadline").

  • Either column flashes the matching sentence in the other column.
  • The tooltip appears near the sentence and goes away on its own.

Summary with "Show in letter"

The summary covers what the letter is, what it asks, the deadline, the documents needed, and who to contact, showing only the items that apply. Each point except "what it is" has a "Show in letter" link.

  • Every point except "what it is" links to at least one highlight.
  • "Show in letter" scrolls to the sentence and flashes it in both columns.

Scam warning

The warning shows before the translation, with specific reasons. It says the app can be wrong and tells the person to contact the agency through its official website. "I understand" reveals the results.

  • The phishing test letter shows reasons before anything else.
  • The translation stays hidden until "I understand" is ticked.
  • Normal official test letters show no warning.

Letter checking (verifier)

A second check confirms that each point and highlight is supported by the original and reviews the scam decision. A point it can't confirm or fix is still shown, labeled "Couldn't verify, check the original."

  • No summary point appears without a matching original sentence unless it carries the label.
  • The label appears in both interface languages.

Start over and Close

Start over clears everything and returns to upload. Close clears everything, then opens cat-bounce.com (demo only). Nothing is saved.

  • No trace of the previous letter remains after Start over.
  • Close clears the screen before redirecting.

Disclosures

A "not legal advice" note always sits under the results. A disclosures section explains the app's limits and that nothing is stored. assumption It opens on the same screen.

  • Available in both interface languages.

States

Informational letterStill explains what the message means (for example, "your application was received") and says no further steps are needed at this time.
Past deadlineThe date is still shown, with a small caution box explaining that it has already passed.
Incomplete letterA small caution box explains that the letter looks incomplete, says what seems to be missing, and suggests checking the upload.
Possible scamWarning and reasons first, then "I understand," then results.
Can't be readBlank, unreadable, not a letter, or not in English. The message asks them to check the file.
Processing errorThe same calm message style, with a Try again option.

Caution boxes use a calm style; red is reserved for scams. Nothing persists. Start over, Close, or closing the tab clears everything.
